## Role

Two "dead" marionettes found by the team deep in the [Night Floors](/locations/the-night-floors/), torn open by buckshot with red tissue paper standing in for blood — carrying wallets with genuine, brand-new-looking 1950s New York identity cards for **Eric K. Carter** (1953 card) and **Ronald Burbach** (1955 card).

## Records Found

- **Eric Kristos Carter** (1924–1955): drafted into the US Army in 1943 at 19, served in the European Theater with a special SS-hunting detachment and rare "theater-wide" clearance; returned home a first lieutenant in 1946; re-upped for Korea in 1950, returned home after a year due to "battle fatigue."
- **Ronald James Burbach** (1924–1955): volunteered for the Marines in 1944 at 20, supported the planned invasion of Japan; returned home a corporal in 1946; reactivated with the 1st Marine Division for Korea, landed at Incheon in 1950, injured and sent home to convalesce. Arrested **1 June 1953** at the American Museum of Natural History for breaking and entering; released the following week on the order of NYPD Commissioner Francis William Holbrooke Adams, for the sole stated reason "decorated veteran." All charges dropped.
- No record the two ever served together, though both were from New York City.
- **9 September 1955**: both men were murdered together at an unnamed hotel — the surviving case file is thin, containing a single crime scene photo showing both men in heavy military jackets with the skin of their faces carefully removed, laid out next to a pile of old gas masks and shotguns. A person of interest is listed: **Virgil U. Griffith**, of White Plains, NY.
