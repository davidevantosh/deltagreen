## Overview

A vast, shifting pocket dimension accessed through the [Macallister Building's](/locations/macallister-building/) roof access door — instead of the roof, the door opens onto an endless hotel-like space of hallways, apartments, and impossible rooms. Time moves differently here: a full night of wandering (12+ hours subjectively) can pass in under a minute of real time outside. Several tenants of the Macallister appear to live here as much as, or instead of, in their actual apartments.

David Langford, a cable repairman trapped here since 20 March 1995, warned the team never to sleep inside — advice they heeded throughout both excursions.

## Notable Areas

### The Smoking Lounge
A turn-of-the-century men's club: velvet wallpaper, a roaring hearth, overstuffed leather armchairs, a wet bar, a walk-in humidor, and floor-to-ceiling rosewood bookcases. First entered via the roof door in Session 4. Home to [Mark Roark](/npcs/suspects-witnesses/mark-roark/) and, later, [Roger Carun](/npcs/suspects-witnesses/roger-carun/) — both found here on separate visits.

### Castaigne's Apartment
Packed wall-to-wall with old newspapers and magazines documenting an alternate 20th-century history — a Russo-Germanic conflict that never occurred in our reality. Home to the night manager, [Castaigne](/npcs/suspects-witnesses/castaigne/).

### The Recursive Room
A locked room containing the only window the team found in the Night Floors. Opening the curtains reveals a living room, whose window opens onto a dining room, then a lounge, then a bedroom, recursing without end.

### The Ballroom
A grand double-doored hall filled with life-size marionettes dancing, serving drinks, and playing music — completely indifferent to the agents. Their strings run up into darkness on self-moving tracks, with no visible operator. The mastiff Abe was recovered here in Session 7.

### The Chasm
A stretch of hallway split by a bottomless, ~100-meter-wide gap. Figures were seen running along the far side before the lights there went out and heavy, unexplained footsteps shook dust from the ceiling.

### The Theater
Revealed when Mortimer found a long crack in the plaster and pulled it apart — an entire hallway turned out to be a plywood stage set, facing a theater packed with hundreds of marionettes in the audience. When the agents fled, the marionettes rose and dispersed into the halls "with silent purpose."

### The Projection Room
Marked "PROJECTION." Contains a giant film projector. Murphy glimpsed a lone figure in gold and silver near the front of an otherwise empty theater beyond, beginning to remove a strange white mask — before the projector shorted out and the room went dark.

### The Wall of Photographs
A wall bearing dozens of photographs of blank-faced people in 1930s dress, each holding an oddly shaped bottle, captioned with names including A. Daribondi, E. Losette, J. Linz, E. Moseby, D. Carver, G. Topchick, and H. Lundine. The final photograph shows only an empty dark bottle on the floor — captioned **Miya Fukazawa**, Agent Marlowe's real name.

## Session Notes

- **Session 4:** First entry, via the roof door. Met Mark Roark and Castaigne; found two marionette "corpses" carrying Carter and Burbach's IDs; witnessed "Agent Vega" swallowed by the floor.
- **Session 5:** Escaped after Mason found the shocking photograph implicating Mortimer and Gloria in the smoking lounge's hidden book; exited to find barely a minute had passed outside.
- **Session 7:** Second entry via a door in Vanfitz's library. Found the wall of photographs, the ballroom, the chasm, the clockwork figure and its invitation, the Yellow Sign newspaper scrap, and the stage-set reveal. Found David Langford.
- **Session 8:** Extended search for an exit — encountered patients in hospital garb, an active Purson summoning ritual, the aged bank officer and projection room, a gunfight's aftermath, and finally the way back out via the smoking lounge, where Roark and Carun were found together.
